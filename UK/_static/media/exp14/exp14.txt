Once, a train could enter through this door. In these rooms—the packing warehouses of the Rozendaal textile factory—supplies were unpacked, and ready goods were loaded. Further along in the museum, you can still see the wooden doors of the loading docks. Outside, there is a pond, but before it was there, a turntable stood in that spot, allowing locomotives and wagons to be rotated.
These warehouses are now exhibition spaces. However, the space you are currently in is also a kind of factory where things are made. First, fossils are extracted from stones in the glass-walled room. Second, the weaving machines produce tea towels and tablecloths, which are sold in the museum shop. Finally, every visitor is encouraged to make something here, as creating with your hands often helps you understand and remember things better!
Outside, this is the only place in Enschede still surrounded by textile factories. To your right, you can see the cotton warehouse of N.J. Menko, once part of Spinning Mill Roombeek. Across the pond is the art academy, now housed in the Tetem 2 building. Opposing Tetem 1 today is Tetem Art Space. The Twentse Textielmaatschappij, or Tetem, produced blankets and, like the factory you are in now, was owned by the Rozendaal company.
