The Museumfabriek houses a vast collection, consisting of perhaps 300,000 objects. These range from photographs, postcards, historical maps, butterflies, beetles, archaeology, textile machines, clothing, fossils, to books. In fact, everything except art.
The museum doesn’t have curators, but it does have a large group of volunteers and researchers who work to digitise the collection. Together, they ensure that the items are photographed, scanned, and documented with descriptive text. The objects, along with their accompanying information, are then available almost immediately online at www.deatlasvanooit.nl.
What you’re looking at now are the researchers’ workspaces. Much of the work is done on the computer, but sometimes the research still requires hands-on effort. All butterflies, for example, need to be examined from every angle before they can be described and photographed.
To make photographing easier, our volunteers have invented a semi-automatic insect photography machine, which you can see in the middle of the room. The space at the back right is the restoration studio of the Rijksmuseum Twenthe. The space at the back left is the workshop of the collection manager. Behind this workshop are the photo studio and the storage rooms of The Museumfabriek, where most of these objects are kept.
