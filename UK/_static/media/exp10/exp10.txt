In Twente and the Achterhoek, you can find fossils in various places, such as clay pits and quarries. One world-famous site is the quarry in Winterswijk. About 250 million years ago, the largest mass extinction – a different term for a great extinction – occurred. Right after this extinction period, many new species of plants and animals began to emerge.
The rock from that time, just after the extinction, comes to the surface in Winterswijk. Back then, it was a coastal plain where, just like now, dead animals such as shells and jellyfish, as well as plants, washed up. And where land animals walked along the shore.
The Museumfabriek's collection includes thousands of fossils from Winterswijk, many of which are still embedded in the rock that was once the beach. In order to study them properly, they need to be carefully removed from the stones. This is done a few times a week in this space: the fossil preparation area.
The next listening point is next to the steam engine. To get there, walk towards the weaving machines. Between the fence and the wall, there is a small staircase leading down. The steam engine is located there.
