Welcome to the audio tour of the Museumfabriek! This tour will guide you through 16 fascinating spots in the museum. Along the way, you’ll receive directions, and at each listening point, you’ll find a QR-code for additional information.  
The place where you are standing now used to be the textile factory "Het Rozendaal." From 1900 onwards, textiles were recycled here. Cotton waste and leftover materials from other factories were brought to this site. Right where you’re standing, machines once stood that broke the cotton scraps apart. In the ducts you see, now covered with glass, were transporting pipes that moved materials from one machine to another.  
Soon, you’ll enter a room with a large brown leather sofa. This is where machines once stood to weave new fabrics from recycled cotton. Since the cotton was no longer very strong, it was used to make cleaning cloths. However, the hall where the exhibition is now located was not a production hall but a storage space.  
It’s hardly visible nowadays, but behind the bar in this reception area was where the steam engine used to be. It powered all the machines in the factory! Outside, you can still see the chimney of the steam boiler.  
A hundred years ago, around 1920, this was already a textile recycling factory. Employees moved around using electric carts—very modern for the time! In the glass booth, there’s a large photo of the factory from 1920. You can clearly see the electric carts in that image.  
Now, walk through the sliding doors past the globe. At the end, turn right. You’ll see a wall of paintings—that’s the next listening point. If there isn’t a group there, feel free to have a look. If the space is occupied, continue straight, turn right, and go down the stairs. Follow the route until you see the mammoth skeleton.
