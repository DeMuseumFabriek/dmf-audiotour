Take a look at the glass display case. Inside, you'll see animals that lived in Twente during the last Ice Age. This Ice Age is called the Weichselian. The Weichselian lasted from 116,000 years ago until about 12,000 years ago.  
The ice sheets and glaciers did not reach the Netherlands, but it was much colder than it is now. Through evolution and natural selection, the best traits of animals are preserved. Sometimes, these are useful traits. For example, when there is often snow, a white coat is very useful because it blends into the white surroundings. Take a look at the Arctic fox and the Arctic hare in the bottom left. A thick coat is, of course, also useful in the cold. The musk ox, up top, has a thick coat.  
On the second row from the bottom, towards the back, you'll find a saiga antelope. It has a very long snout. This means the cold air must travel a long way through its nose, warming up a little before it reaches its lungs. This is another adaptation to a cold climate.  
Evolution doesn’t just result in useful traits, as shown by the giant deer. It had antlers that could be 3.5 meters wide! Above in the display case, you'll see a real antler. The skeleton on the left is a replica. Such a large antler isn’t very practical. But the bigger the antler, the healthier the animal. Only a healthy deer can grow such a large antler. A deer with large antlers is more attractive to female deer. The calves of these deer inherit the trait for large antlers, so they too grow big antlers.
