This model was built in the 1970s and 1980s and shows the spinning mill Jannink on Haaksbergerstraat in Enschede as it looked around 1920. Inside the factory, you can see all stages of the spinning process, from cotton bale to thread. A single steam engine drives all the machines in the various departments via belts and shafts. The coal bunkers, offices, and storage rooms are also included in the model. People who worked in this factory in the 1960s recalled that there was a laboratory above the dispatch area where quality tests were carried out. There, they checked whether the cotton delivered met the required standards and whether the finished products met quality requirements.
The factory was built in 1900 and was ordered from a catalogue. All the parts could be ordered, along with the blueprints and building plans. Even machines and sprinkler systems could be delivered from England. Only a contractor and an architect were needed to build the factory according to the plans. And, of course, bricks were needed. That’s why Jannink bought a clay pit and brickworks nearby to ensure there would be no shortage of bricks for the factory’s construction.
