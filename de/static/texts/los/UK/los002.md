###### Name Loose Cover 

The name Los Hoes comes from the Lower Saxon lös hoes (plural: lösse huze).

It probably means “open house”, because in the past there was only one large room where people and animals slept together under one roof.

In winter it was nice and warm.

The back part of the house near the large doors was called the part and was for the animals and work.

The front part was the living area.

It is sometimes thought that the name also refers to the kit nature of the house (loose = not fixed).

The wooden beams were often numbered and connected with mortise and tenon joints.

This means that the building was sometimes demolished and rebuilt elsewhere, for example when the soil was exhausted and the farmer found a better piece of land.

As the family grew or the business went well, additional anchor trusses could be purchased.

This way the farm was made longer.

There was a lively trade there.
