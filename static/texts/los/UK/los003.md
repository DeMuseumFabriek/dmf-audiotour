###### Facade sign

If you look at the point of the roof, you will see a gable sign.

Gable signs were originally created by the crossing wind springs, the planks between the facade and the roof that were supposed to keep the wind out.

The ends were often decorated with animal heads, usually horse heads, because the horse was the farmer's most important asset.

In popular culture, the horse was also seen as a symbol of fertility or as protection against disaster.

Traditionally, facade signs mainly had a disaster-preventing function.

Farmers, as it were, asked for help from supernatural forces to prevent bad luck and disaster.

Life was often hard in Twente.

The soil was usually poor sandy soil, alternating with small fertile areas in the stream valleys.

A bad harvest due to drought, rain or diseases could lead to hunger.

Natural disasters, wars or livestock diseases could also cause major problems.

In addition, people believed in white women, hell hounds and evil spirits, who had to be kept out.

For food, rye was the staple food, but the ergot fungus sometimes occurred in it.

Those who ate this could hallucinate, which may have contributed to stories of haunting ghosts.

Facade signs changed over time.

If you want to know how they changed, look at the **Atlas of Once**.
