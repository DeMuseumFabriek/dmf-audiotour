###### Tools from flax to yarn

Making linen from flax was a long and difficult process.

First the soil was prepared and the flax seed was sown.

About 100 days later the flax could be harvested and dried, as in the flax chapel.

The flax seed bulb then had to be separated from the stem, this is called repelling.

Sometimes the stems were left on the land or immersed for a period of time to rot.

Retting means that the bark separates from the fiber because the adhesive dissolves pectin.

The retting took 1 to 2 weeks, smelled and polluted the water.

Then the flax was dried again.

A few more steps were needed to spin the flax into yarn:

• Vomiting: the bark was broken into pieces.

• Scutching: last pieces of bark were removed.

• Casting: the fibers were laid parallel and lint was removed.

Many of the appliances used for this can be seen on the top right of the shelf, just below the attic.

Then came spinning: the fibers were sometimes tied in advance on a distaff or pulled directly from the bunch of fibers and spun into thread.

The yarn was wound onto bobbins, ready for the loom.

Spinning was usually the work of women and children.

The spinning wheel replaced the spindle and spinning stones around 1300.

This allowed the farmer's wife to make thread faster and more evenly.
