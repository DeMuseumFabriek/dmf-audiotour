###### Flax chapel 

You see a flax chapel here.

Before flax was dried, it was usually placed upright, then it was called a chapel.

Flax grows almost everywhere, so it was an easy crop on the poor Twente soil.

It was mainly cultivated for linen, for sheets, clothes and other fabrics.

The fibers were removed from the stems.

You can see the fibers in the chest next to the flax chapel; they feel a bit like hair.

The flax seed bulb contains light brown seeds.

This was used to make linseed oil, which was used to protect wood against wind and weather.

But linseed and cold-pressed oil were also eaten, for example during Lent as a substitute for meat.

There are two types of flax: fiber flax for linen and the shorter oil flax for oil.

Fiber flax can also be used for oil, but yields much less than oil flax.

The number 100 plays an important role in flax.

Sowing took place around the hundredth day of the year.

In June it bloomed with small blue flowers.

The flax could be harvested a hundred days after sowing.

The entire plant was removed from the ground by root, because the fiber extends into the roots.

This gave them long fibers to make textiles.

Fun fact: in the first versions of Sleeping Beauty she didn't prick herself on a spinning wheel, but on a straw.

Flax straws are hard pieces of the stem.
