###### Food and drinks

In the 17th century, meals were little varied.

The residents of Los Hoes mainly ate what their orchard, vegetable garden and arable land provided. They also often kept bees.

Farmers needed a lot of energy, mainly from carbohydrates: rye bread and thin beer.

Thin beer has a low alcohol content.

It was made by diluting strong beer with water, or by brewing malt a second time.

Beer was safer to drink than water because bacteria were killed during brewing.

Vegetables they ate included legumes, parsnips, turnips, cabbage, onions and leeks.

In November a pig was slaughtered and the meat dried, salted, smoked or pickled.

This way the farmer had meat for several months, even in winter.

(Old) chickens and geese were also eaten.

In the 18th century, beer porridge was made for breakfast from thin beer, old bread, butter, salt, sugar and a little crushed cloves.

Potatoes only appeared on the menu at the end of the 18th century.
