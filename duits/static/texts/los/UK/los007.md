###### Pigs, horses and chickens 

In addition to cows, a farmer usually also had a pig, some chickens, sheep and sometimes a horse.

Pigs were mainly kept for manure and meat.

In the winter they were kept inside a stable in Los Hoes.

They pooped and peeed in one place, and that had to be cleaned up almost daily.

In spring, summer and autumn they could walk outside in the yard or in a fenced piece of land.

There they could dig and root, which was good for their health, food and also for the farmer because they ate plant remains and weeds.

A pig was often slaughtered in November.

The meat was dried, salted, smoked or pickled, so that the farmer had meat for several months, even in winter.

Chickens and sheep were usually kept in a barn.

Chickens often walked where they wanted and sometimes even entered the farm.

Sheep grew a thick undercoat in winter, which after shearing was used to produce wool for clothing and blankets.

The coarser outer coat was used for rope or heavier materials.

A horse was important for work on the land: plowing, transporting and other heavy jobs.

Often a horse was shared with a few farmers.

The animals were therefore important for food, manure and work.

Keeping animals required a lot of attention, especially in the cold winter months.
