###### Cows and deep litter

During the winter the cows were kept in a deep deep stable.

In a deep litter stable, the manure was not removed daily, but slowly “potted up”.

The farmer placed fresh layers of litter over the manure, usually heather sods.

These are pieces of heathland with roots and a layer of sand, which absorbed the manure and urine.

This created a thick, moist layer that served as a bedding for the cows.

Over the course of the winter, this layer could grow up to a meter high.

In the spring the stable was completely emptied and the well-matured manure was used as natural fertilizer on the land.

This is how the poor sandy soil became fertile and the typical ash landscape of Twente was created. It took a long time before that piece of heathland had recovered sufficiently to be able to cut sods there again.
