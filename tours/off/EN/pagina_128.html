The Ommerschans Sword

The Ommerschans sword is one of the most important finds from the Dutch Bronze Age. It was discovered in 1896 in a peat bog, along with bronze chisels, a razor, and whetstones. The sword is over three thousand years old, approximately 68 centimeters long, and heavy. It was unsharpened and unsuitable as a weapon. Archaeologists therefore consider it a ceremonial object. It belongs to a small group of six so-called giant swords in Europe. They are nearly identical and likely made in the same workshop. The objects were deliberately left in the bog, possibly as an offering to higher powers.
