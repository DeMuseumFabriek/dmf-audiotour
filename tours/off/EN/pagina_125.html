Three flint axes from Collendoorn

In Collendoorn, near Hardenberg, archaeologists discovered three large flint axes. They date from the Funnel Beaker Period, between 3400 and 2750 BC. The axes were found close together on the edge of a wetland. No traces of habitation or a grave were found. Therefore, researchers believe this location was deliberately chosen to leave the axes behind. The axes are remarkably large and likely originate from northern Germany or southern Scandinavia. They are too large for normal use and show no signs of wear. They were likely used in ceremonies and then placed in the landscape as offerings. Such multiple finds are rare in the Netherlands.
