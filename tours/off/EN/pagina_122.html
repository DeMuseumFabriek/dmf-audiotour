The Barger-Oosterveld Temple

Here you see a replica of the Barger-Oosterveld temple. It stood here around 1250 BC, in the middle of the raised bog. Such buildings are rare in Europe. The temple measured two by two meters. It consisted of eight oak poles on wide planks. Beams with horn-shaped ends lay on top. Horns were an important symbol. They were associated with fertility, but may also refer to cows, which were sacrificed in the peat. In the center was likely a plank for sacrifices. The temple was later deliberately demolished, and a piece was recovered from the peat. This indicates an important ritual function.
