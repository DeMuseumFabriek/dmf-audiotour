Germanic gold necklaces from Olst.

These four gold necklaces were found in 1952 along the IJssel River near Olst. They date from around 400 AD. The rings are made from melted Roman gold coins, called solidi. The gold is 22 carats. The rings are thickened in the center and decorated with dots and circles. Such motifs refer to eternity in Germanic traditions. This type of jewelry was a sign of power and was worn by prominent leaders. Why the rings ended up in the ground is uncertain. Perhaps they were hidden during a time of unrest. It could also be a sacrifice to ask for protection.
