Roman millstones from Haaksbergen

Roman millstones from Haaksbergen.

These two millstones date from the Roman period, between the 1st and 3rd centuries AD. They were discovered in 1933 at a depth of 2.5 meters in the Haaksbergerveen. The stones are made of basalt lava, a hard rock suitable for grinding grain. The set consists of a lower stone with one hole and an upper stone with two holes. The smaller hole contained a handle. By rotating the upper stone, the grain was ground between the stones. The flour emerged around the edge. These types of hand mills were indispensable in daily life for making bread and porridge.
