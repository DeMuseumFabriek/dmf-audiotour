###### Sleeping, washing and toilet

To the left of the weaving room you will see a box bed.

Under the parents' box bed was a drawer where the baby could sleep.

The box bed may seem short to us, but that was for two reasons:

1\.	People used to be shorter on average.

2\.	There was superstition: it was thought that sleeping lying down was not good, because only the dead lie stretched out. That's why adults often slept sitting down.

The children slept several at a time in a box bed on the other side of the house.

If there was a servant, he usually slept in the attic, above the animals.

There was no bathroom in a Los Hoes.

People had to wash in the laundry room with a bucket, wash basin or lamp bowl.

In the summer this was possible in the stream.

Because a Los Hoes was poorly insulated, it was cold inside in the winter, and washing was usually a chore.

The first water pumps were in the 17th century, but in the countryside from the 18th century. But piped water arrived much later in rural areas than in cities due to the high construction costs.

The construction of municipal and provincial water pipes started between 1892 (Enschede) and 1932 (Borne).

In rural areas, hand pumps sometimes remained in use until 1950–1960.

A Los Hoes also had no toilet.

Outside was a “huuske”, a wooden house with a plank with a hole and a barrel or bucket underneath.

Old newspapers were used as toilet paper.

The barrel had to be emptied regularly into the manure pile.

The work was unpleasant, it smelled and attracted many flies.

In Twente, the sewer was first installed in the big cities, and only around 2008 also in the small hamlets.
